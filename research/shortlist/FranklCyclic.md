# How far can pairing by adding activities take us?

Choose any integer $k\ge 2$ and set $n=k+3$. Place $n$ activities on a cycle. Allow every consecutive block of $k$ activities, every union of such blocks, and the empty scope. Every activity occurs in more than half the scopes. Yet for every activity $a$, some scopes missing $a$ cannot simultaneously be paired with distinct supersets containing $a$. In particular, raising the minimum nonempty scope size cannot rescue this pairing strategy.

## Reading off the family

The complements of the generators are exactly the consecutive triples. A nonempty union of generators has complement equal to an intersection of one or more of these triples. An intersection of distinct triples has at most two vertices. If it contains two, they must be neighbours. Indeed, for $n\ge 5$, a consecutive triple containing two non-neighbours must use the unique two-edge path between them, so that pair occurs together in at most one triple. This includes the pentagon.

Conversely, neighbouring triples give any prescribed consecutive pair as their intersection, the three triples containing a given vertex intersect in that singleton, and intersecting all triples leaves the empty set. The complements of our nonempty scopes are therefore the $n$ consecutive triples, the $n$ consecutive pairs, the $n$ singletons and the empty set. The family has $3n+2$ members including the empty scope. Its smallest nonempty members have size $n-3=k$. Each activity appears in $(n-3)+(n-2)+(n-1)+1=3n-5$ members. Exactly seven scopes omit a given activity, whatever the cycle length. The fraction containing it is $1-7/(3n+2)$, which approaches one. Any two activities can be distinguished by an allowed scope that omits just one of them.

## Four into three

Fix any activity $a$. Remove $a$ and its two neighbours $l,r$ from the ground set. What remains is a consecutive generator $P$ of size $k$. The four scopes missing $a$ that contain $P$ are $P$, $P\cup\{l\}$, $P\cup\{r\}$, and $P\cup\{l,r\}$. Any pairing that adds activities must send each of these to a scope containing both $P$ and $a$.

There are just three such target scopes. The fourth possible candidate, $P\cup\{a\}$, has complement $\{l,r\}$. These two vertices are not neighbours, so this candidate is unavailable. Four sources cannot have distinct representatives among three targets. The choice of $a$ was arbitrary.

## Examples and credit

For $k=2$ we recover the pentagon built from edges. The July 2026 note by makoto-developer gives this obstruction, its 4-to-3 witness, and the extension to edge-generated cycles of length at least five. The note omits the empty scope, giving 10 appearances in 16 scopes for each activity in its pentagon. Our convention includes the empty scope, giving 10 in 17. No source or target in the obstruction changes. [Pentagon example](https://blog.makoto-developer.net/articles/2026-07-23-union-closed-conjecture-3)

For $k=3$ the six-cycle generated by triples has 20 scopes. Every activity occurs in 13. These are distinguishable activities, and the family contains no singleton or two-element scope. The parameterised construction adapts the pentagon witness to allow arbitrarily large minimum scopes. We make no priority claim for the adaptation. Hall's 1935 theorem gives the general necessary-and-sufficient condition for distinct representatives. Only its immediate counting obstruction is needed in this proof. [Hall's paper](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/s1-10.37.26)

Among finite families with no one- or two-element member, six is the least support size that can obstruct containment pairings for every activity. The reproducible exhaustive check `check_scope_matchings.py` tests all full-support labelled families of orders 3, 4, and 5, respectively 2, 32, and 9,890. Every family has a target with a containment matching. It enumerates every subset of the eligible proper scopes, adds the full scope (forced by union closure and full support), checks union closure, and uses augmenting paths to test for a matching. Support of order one or two is impossible in this class. The nonempty support condition excludes the empty ground set.

The accompanying Lean file only treats $k=3$. It models the family by the consecutive windows of lengths 3, 4 and 5, the full set and the empty set. Its statements cover closure under union, the counts, the four-versus-three obstruction and the impossibility of any containment pairing. The proof above identifies these windows with the generator closure and treats all $k\ge 2$. The ordinary Frankl inequality holds for every activity in these examples.
